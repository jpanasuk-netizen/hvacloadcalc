What Size Air Conditioner Do I Need for 2,000 Sq Ft?

2,000 sq ft is the top boundary on the room-AC chart: 30,000 BTU/hr below it and 34,000 above it.

Full breakdown: BTU per square foot.

Quick answer: 2,000 sq ft is the line between 30,000 BTU/hr (1,500 up to 2,000 sq ft) and 34,000 BTU/hr (2,000 up to 2,500 sq ft). 34,000 is the highest capacity on the published chart. Use 30,000 when the area is heavily shaded. Use 34,000 when it is very sunny or more than two people are there regularly. A central system for a whole house is a load calculation, not this row.

This is the last boundary the chart publishes. There is no row above 2,000 up to 2,500 sq ft and no capacity above 34,000 BTU/hr in the table. A 2,000 sq ft area cooled as one room-AC zone can use the two rows. A 2,000 sq ft house on a central system cannot. The product type in the source is a room air conditioner. Treating 34,000 as a central-system answer invents a use the chart does not state.

How to size it

If you are still on the room-AC question, the row gap is 4,000 BTU/hr. That matches the kitchen adder's size, so keep the reasons separate. Shade selects 30,000 and then takes 10% off. Sun or extra regular occupants select 34,000 and then apply their own adjustment. A kitchen adder is a third fact, not a synonym for "buy the higher row."

10% of 30,000 BTU/hr is 3,000 BTU/hr (30,000 × 10%).

10% of 34,000 BTU/hr is 3,400 BTU/hr (34,000 × 10%).

Shaded lower path: 30,000 − 10% = 27,000 BTU/hr (10% of 30,000 is 3,000). Sunny upper path: 34,000 + 10% = 37,400 BTU/hr (10% of 34,000 is 3,400). One person beyond two on the upper row: 34,000 + 600 = 34,600 BTU/hr. Kitchen on the lower row: 30,000 + 4,000 = 34,000 BTU/hr, which equals the higher row's printed capacity only as a sum. Kitchen on the upper row: 34,000 + 4,000 = 38,000 BTU/hr. Use the upper-row kitchen sum only when the area both earned 34,000 and the unit is used in a kitchen.

"An oversized air conditioner costs more to purchase, wastes energy and does not provide better cooling." At this boundary the costly version is buying 34,000 for a shaded area whose chart path is 30,000 minus 10%.

The ceiling assumption does not get looser because the area is large. It is still an 8-foot ceiling, and a higher ceiling still means you may want a room air conditioner with a higher CADR.

Frequently asked questions

What size air conditioner for 2,000 sq ft?

1,500 up to 2,000 sq ft is 30,000 BTU/hr, and 2,000 up to 2,500 sq ft is 34,000 BTU/hr. Heavy shade is the case for 30,000. A very sunny area, or more than two people regularly, is the case for 34,000.

Is 34,000 BTU/hr the largest figure on the chart?

34,000 BTU/hr is the capacity printed for 2,000 up to 2,500 sq ft, which is the highest row in the table. The boundary step into that row is 4,000 BTU/hr (34,000 − 30,000).

What do the four adjustments do at this size?

Shade on 30,000 subtracts 10% (3,000) and lands at 27,000 BTU/hr. Sun on 34,000 adds 10% (3,400) and lands at 37,400 BTU/hr. Each person beyond two adds 600 BTU/hr. A kitchen adds 4,000 BTU/hr.

Should a 2,000 sq ft house use this chart for central air?

No. The table sizes a room air conditioner and assumes an 8-foot ceiling. A whole-house central system should get a load calculation on the HVAC load calculator. Higher ceilings: select a room air conditioner with a higher CADR.

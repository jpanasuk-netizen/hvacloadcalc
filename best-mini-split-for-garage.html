Best Mini Split for a Garage: Size It First

Published November 24, 2026

The best mini split for your garage is first the right size. Enter the garage's floor and ceiling; this page returns ENERGY STAR's capacity row. It does not rank brands.

Quick answer: The best mini split for a garage starts with the right size. ENERGY STAR's chart calls for 10,000 BTU/h for 400 to 450 sq ft, 12,000 for 450 to 550 and 14,000 for 550 to 700, calculated for an 8-foot ceiling. Add 10% for a very sunny space, and consider more capacity for higher ceilings.

The rows, the 8-foot ceiling basis and the 10% sunny adjustment are from ENERGY STAR's room air conditioner sizing chart, read November 24, 2026. It is a room AC chart; this page applies it to a garage floor area and says where that falls short.

Garage sizer

Enter the inside length and width of the garage and the ceiling height. The sizer multiplies length by width (derived), finds the ENERGY STAR row for that area, and adds 10% if you tick very sunny.

ENERGY STAR's other adjustments: reduce 10% for a heavily shaded room, add 600 BTUs for each person beyond two who regularly uses the room, and add 4,000 BTUs in a kitchen. The +10% column is arithmetic on this page. Every capacity row here is in our ENERGY STAR sizing data.

Why a garage is not a bedroom

The chart assumes a room inside a house. A garage often has an uninsulated door, thin walls and a higher ceiling, and ENERGY STAR's own note is that higher ceilings may call for more capacity. Treat the row as the floor for a well-sealed garage, and get a full load calculation for one with little insulation. For a rough rule of thumb, see how many BTU per square foot you need.

Heating a garage in winter? Check heat pump sizing too. Compare models by efficiency in the mini split SEER2 chart. For rooms other than the garage, use the mini split room size calculator; the room-size versions of these rows are on what size air conditioner for a 400 sq ft room and 500 sq ft room.

What size AC unit do I need for 1,000 square feet?

This calculator’s baseline is 1,000 × 25 BTU/hr, before ceiling, sun, insulation, and windows.

Quick answer: 1,000 × 25 = 25,000 BTU/hr on this calculator, before the other factors. DOE’s heating and cooling guide defines one ton as 12,000 Btu, so 25,000 ÷ 12,000 = 2.08 tons. That division is not a model number to order.

Step 1: the base load

1,000 sq ft × 25 BTU/hr per sq ft = 25,000 BTU/hr before ceiling, sun, insulation, and windows. With DOE’s 12,000 Btu per ton, 25,000 ÷ 12,000 = 2.08 tons.

25 BTU/hr per square foot is this calculator’s baseline.

The oversizing trap

An oversized unit can satisfy the thermostat before it has dehumidified, and leave the space cold and clammy. This page does not print a percent of oversize that is safe.

Ducts and airflow (central only)

Airflow is CFM = BTU/hr ÷ (ΔT × 1.08), on the duct CFM guide. This page does not assign a CFM-per-ton rule.

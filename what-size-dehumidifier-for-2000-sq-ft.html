What Size Dehumidifier Do I Need for 2,000 Sq Ft?

Last updated: October 7, 2026

2,000 sq ft is the line between the under-2,000 pint ranges and the over-2,000 pint floors.

Quick answer: ENERGY STAR sizes dehumidifiers in two columns: under 2,000 sq ft, and over 2,000 sq ft. A space of 2,000 sq ft is the line between them. The large-room column is 30+ pints per 24 hours for slight to moderate dampness, 40+ for a very damp space, and 50+ for a wet one. The chart's own tie-break is that it is better to oversize than undersize.

How to size it

The source writes the split as a strict inequality: small-to-medium is under 2,000 sq ft, and large is over 2,000 sq ft. A room measured at 2,000 sq ft is not inside either phrase. That is the reason to use the sentence ENERGY STAR prints in bold on the same page: "It's better to oversize than undersize!" Read 2,000 sq ft from the large-room floors (30+, 40+, 50+) rather than from the smaller ranges.

Pick the floor from the condition, not from the square footage alone. Slightly to moderately damp, musty, 50-75% RH: 30+ pints per 24 hours. Very damp, with damp spots and 75-90% RH: 40+ pints. Wet, with sweating surfaces, seepage, or laundry drying, at 90-100% RH: 50+ pints. The search under the answer is a 50 pint ENERGY STAR unit because that meets the wet large-room floor, and a drier large room that only needed 30+ or 40+ is the case the oversize rule already accepts.

Do not use this column for a space that is under 2,000 sq ft. Those ranges are 20-30, 25-40, and 30-50, and the basement page walks the under-2,000 column, including the cold-room frost warning. Below 65°F the same frost note still applies here: consider a model specified for lower temperatures or one with an anti-frost sensor.

The RH you are aiming at, once a unit is running, is generally 30-50%. In a cold climate during the heating season that tightens to 30-40% so windows do not condense. Keep doors and windows closed while it runs. ENERGY STAR certified dehumidifiers use 20% less energy than conventional units; the metric is integrated energy factor in L/kWh, and no single target number is published.

If RH is consistently above 55% in a humid climate and the home has central air conditioning, the same source points to a whole-home dehumidifier. That is a different machine from a 50 pint portable. It is also a different question from the room air conditioner chart, which tells you not to oversize cooling equipment. The dehumidifier sentence and the air conditioner sentence are both in ENERGY STAR's pages, and they do not cancel each other. Cooling capacity for the house is a load calculation.

Worked example: a 2,000 sq ft basement

  1. Measure: 40 × 50 ft is 2,000 sq ft, exactly on ENERGY STAR's line between the two columns.
  2. Read the condition: a hygrometer shows 80% RH and there are damp spots on the walls. That is ENERGY STAR's "very damp" row, 75-90% RH.
  3. Pick the column: the under-2,000 range is 25-40 pints and the over-2,000 floor is 40+. Because the area is on the line and ENERGY STAR says it is better to oversize than undersize, shop 40+ pints per day.
  4. Check the temperature: if the basement sits below 65°F in spring or fall, frost can form on the coils. Look for a model specified for lower temperatures or one with an anti-frost sensor.
  5. Set the target: once it runs, set the humidistat in the 30-50% range, or 30-40% during the heating season in a cold climate to avoid window condensation.

If you are replacing a dehumidifier that already runs in this space, ENERGY STAR says you can judge the size by how much water you empty each day.

Common mistakes at 2,000 sq ft

  • Reading the smaller column because the area is "not over 2,000." The source splits at under and over 2,000. On the line, its oversize rule points to the large-room floors.
  • Running with doors and windows open. ENERGY STAR says doors and windows to the space should be closed while the unit runs.
  • Pushing the unit against a wall. If the model does not discharge air from the top, ENERGY STAR says to keep it away from walls and furniture so air can circulate.
  • Running a long drain hose across the floor. ENERGY STAR says to place the unit close enough to the floor drain or sump to avoid a long, unwieldy hose, and to keep drain hoses away from electrical cords and connections.
  • Comparing an old pint rating with a new one. ENERGY STAR notes that capacity options have changed for dehumidifiers. Compare current ratings with current ratings, and use the chart above for the size.
  • Expecting one portable to dry a whole 2,000 sq ft house. ENERGY STAR describes portables as typically used for a single room or space. Whole-home units use the home's air ducts to dehumidify one or more rooms.

Frequently asked questions

What size dehumidifier for 2,000 sq ft?

The large-room column, for spaces over 2,000 sq ft, is 30+ pints per 24 hours when slightly to moderately damp, 40+ when very damp, and 50+ when wet. A space sitting on 2,000 sq ft matches neither the under column nor the over column. Because it is better to oversize than undersize, read the large-room floors.

What are the under-2,000 numbers for comparison?

The small-to-medium column, under 2,000 sq ft, is 20-30, 25-40, and 30-50 pints per 24 hours for the same three conditions. A space that is actually under 2,000 sq ft should use that column, not the large-room floors.

Which dampness description matches which large-room floor?

Musty odor and 50-75% RH is 30+ pints. A consistent damp smell, damp spots, and 75-90% RH is 40+ pints. Sweating walls or floors, seepage, or laundry drying, at 90-100% RH, is 50+ pints. The indoor target is generally 30-50% RH.

How is this different from sizing a 2,000 sq ft air conditioner?

The room air conditioner chart warns that an oversized unit wastes energy. The dehumidifier chart says it is better to oversize than undersize. They are different products. Central cooling is still a load calculation on the HVAC load calculator. Below 65°F, watch for frost on dehumidifier coils.

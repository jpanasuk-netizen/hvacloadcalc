What Size Air Conditioner Do I Need for a 500 Sq Ft Room?

500 sq ft is inside the 12,000 BTU/hr row, between 450 and 550 sq ft.

Full breakdown: BTU per square foot.

Quick answer: A 500 sq ft room falls in ENERGY STAR's 450 up to 550 sq ft row, which needs 12,000 BTU/hr. That is the capacity to adjust. The next published capacity, 14,000 BTU/hr, starts at 550 up to 700 sq ft and is not the sunny version of this room.

Because 500 sq ft is not an endpoint, you do not flip a coin between neighboring rows. Measure length times width. If the area is still at least 450 and short of 550 sq ft, the row remains 12,000 BTU/hr. Crossing 550 sq ft is what opens the 14,000 row. A sunny exposure is a percent, not a new area.

How to size it

Adjust 12,000 BTU/hr in place. A very sunny room adds 10%. A heavily shaded room subtracts 10%. Extra people and a kitchen are flat additions, and they can combine with one of the percent cases because they describe different facts about the room.

AdjustmentOn the 12,000 rowSource
Heavily shaded, reduce capacity 10%12,000 − 10% = 10,800 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Very sunny, increase capacity 10%12,000 + 10% = 13,200 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Each additional person beyond two12,000 + 600 BTU/hr = 12,600 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Unit used in a kitchen12,000 + 4,000 BTU/hr = 16,000 BTU/hrENERGY STAR

10% of 12,000 BTU/hr is 1,200 BTU/hr (12,000 × 10%).

The sunny result is 13,200 BTU/hr, still under the 14,000 capacity that ENERGY STAR assigns to a larger area. The shaded result is 10,800 BTU/hr, which is not a reason to shop the 10,000 row from 400 up to 450 sq ft. One person beyond two makes 12,000 + 600 = 12,600 BTU/hr. Kitchen use makes 12,000 + 4,000 = 16,000 BTU/hr. A sunny kitchen would be 12,000 + 10% + 4,000, or 13,200 + 4,000 = 17,200 BTU/hr. Stack only the facts you actually have.

Once the unit is in, ENERGY STAR's operating tip is to set the thermostat as high as comfortable, typically 78°F. That setpoint does not resize the row. It is how the 12,000 BTU/hr unit is meant to be run.

The row also assumes an 8-foot ceiling. A higher ceiling is the case for a higher CADR, using ENERGY STAR's word, rather than a silent upgrade to 14,000.

Frequently asked questions

What size air conditioner for a 500 sq ft room?

The matching ENERGY STAR row is 450 up to 550 sq ft, at 12,000 BTU/hr. A 500 sq ft room is inside that range, so you do not choose between two capacities the way a boundary room does.

Does a sunny 500 sq ft room need the 14,000 BTU/hr row?

A very sunny room increases the 12,000 figure by 10%. Ten percent of 12,000 is 1,200, so the sunny target is 13,200 BTU/hr. The 14,000 capacity belongs to 550 up to 700 sq ft. Sun does not reassign the floor area.

What thermostat setting does ENERGY STAR pair with a room unit?

Set the thermostat as high as comfortable, typically 78°F. That tip does not change the 12,000 BTU/hr row. People still add 600 BTU/hr each beyond two, and a kitchen still adds 4,000 BTU/hr.

Is 12,000 BTU/hr a whole-house answer?

It is a room air conditioner capacity on an 8-foot ceiling. A central system needs the HVAC load calculator. If the ceiling is higher, select a room air conditioner with a higher CADR.

What Size Air Conditioner Do I Need for a 300 Sq Ft Room?

300 sq ft is the line between 7,000 and 8,000 BTU/hr, and occupancy is often what decides it.

Full breakdown: BTU per square foot.

Quick answer: A 300 sq ft room is the boundary between 7,000 BTU/hr (250 up to 300 sq ft) and 8,000 BTU/hr (300 up to 350 sq ft). Keep 7,000 when the room is heavily shaded and only two people use it. Move to 8,000 when the room is very sunny or more than two people are there regularly, then add 600 BTU/hr for each person past two.

Rooms around this floor area are often the ones that break the two-person assumption: a primary bedroom, a home office that hosts a third person, or a den. ENERGY STAR's people rule is specific. More than two people regularly in the room means add 600 BTU/hr per additional person. It does not mean "add 600 once and also jump a row." If the extra person is the reason you already chose 8,000, the adder goes on top of 8,000.

How to size it

Decide the row from shade, sun, and whether more than two people use the room. Then run the matching adjustment. The two capacities are 1,000 BTU/hr apart (8,000 − 7,000), which is more than the 700 BTU/hr shade cut on the lower row.

AdjustmentOn the 7,000 rowOn the 8,000 rowSource
Heavily shaded, reduce capacity 10%7,000 − 10% = 6,300 BTU/hr8,000 − 10% = 7,200 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Very sunny, increase capacity 10%7,000 + 10% = 7,700 BTU/hr8,000 + 10% = 8,800 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Each additional person beyond two7,000 + 600 BTU/hr = 7,600 BTU/hr8,000 + 600 BTU/hr = 8,600 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Unit used in a kitchen7,000 + 4,000 BTU/hr = 11,000 BTU/hr8,000 + 4,000 BTU/hr = 12,000 BTU/hrENERGY STAR

10% of 7,000 BTU/hr is 700 BTU/hr (7,000 × 10%).

10% of 8,000 BTU/hr is 800 BTU/hr (8,000 × 10%).

Heavy shade and two or fewer people: 7,000 minus 10% = 6,300 BTU/hr. Very sunny, before any extra person: 8,000 plus 10% = 8,800 BTU/hr. One person beyond two, on the upper row and without stacking sun: 8,000 + 600 = 8,600 BTU/hr. A kitchen use is the large step, 4,000 BTU/hr, so 11,000 from the low row or 12,000 from the high row. Take that step only when the unit is actually used in a kitchen.

None of those results assume a ceiling above 8-foot. If yours is higher, ENERGY STAR's instruction is to select a room air conditioner with a higher CADR. There is no published percent per extra foot on this chart.

Frequently asked questions

What size air conditioner for a 300 sq ft room?

250 up to 300 sq ft is 7,000 BTU/hr. 300 up to 350 sq ft is 8,000 BTU/hr. A heavily shaded room can stay at 7,000. A very sunny room, or more than two people regularly, should start at 8,000.

How do I add a third regular occupant at 300 sq ft?

The chart covers up to two people. Each person beyond two adds 600 BTU/hr. If occupancy is why you chose the 8,000 row, one extra person is 8,000 + 600 = 8,600 BTU/hr. Do not also switch rows a second time.

What is the shaded result on the lower row?

Start at 7,000 and reduce capacity 10%. Ten percent of 7,000 is 700, so 7,000 − 700 = 6,300 BTU/hr. That is the heavy-shade case, not a reason to quote the 6,000 row from a smaller area.

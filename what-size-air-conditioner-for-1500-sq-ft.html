What Size Air Conditioner Do I Need for 1,500 Sq Ft?

1,500 sq ft is a 6,000 BTU/hr step on the room-AC chart, from 24,000 up to 30,000.

Full breakdown: BTU per square foot.

For a smaller whole space on a different method, see what size AC unit for 1,000 sq ft. The numbers below are only the ENERGY STAR room air conditioner rows at 1,500 sq ft.

Quick answer: 1,500 sq ft is the boundary between 24,000 BTU/hr (1,400 up to 1,500 sq ft) and 30,000 BTU/hr (1,500 up to 2,000 sq ft). The step is 6,000 BTU/hr. Heavy shade stays with 24,000. A very sunny area, or more than two people regularly, moves to 30,000.

Put the step in scale. 30,000 minus 24,000 is 6,000 BTU/hr, and 6,000 BTU/hr is the whole capacity ENERGY STAR assigns to a 150 up to 250 sq ft room. Choosing the wrong side of 1,500 sq ft is not a small rounding error. It is an entire small-room unit of capacity. That is also why a central system for a 1,500 sq ft house should not be picked off this window-unit table.

How to size it

If the area is one room-sized zone cooled by room equipment, pick a side. Shade and ordinary occupancy: 24,000. Very sunny, or more than two people regularly: 30,000. Then apply the adjustment that matches, once.

AdjustmentOn the 24,000 rowOn the 30,000 rowSource
Heavily shaded, reduce capacity 10%24,000 − 10% = 21,600 BTU/hr30,000 − 10% = 27,000 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Very sunny, increase capacity 10%24,000 + 10% = 26,400 BTU/hr30,000 + 10% = 33,000 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Each additional person beyond two24,000 + 600 BTU/hr = 24,600 BTU/hr30,000 + 600 BTU/hr = 30,600 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Unit used in a kitchen24,000 + 4,000 BTU/hr = 28,000 BTU/hr30,000 + 4,000 BTU/hr = 34,000 BTU/hrENERGY STAR

10% of 24,000 BTU/hr is 2,400 BTU/hr (24,000 × 10%).

10% of 30,000 BTU/hr is 3,000 BTU/hr (30,000 × 10%).

Shaded lower path: 24,000 − 10% = 21,600 BTU/hr, because 10% of 24,000 is 2,400. Sunny upper path: 30,000 + 10% = 33,000 BTU/hr, because 10% of 30,000 is 3,000. One person beyond two on the upper row is 30,000 + 600 = 30,600 BTU/hr. Kitchen use on the lower row is 24,000 + 4,000 = 28,000 BTU/hr. Kitchen use on the upper row is 30,000 + 4,000 = 34,000 BTU/hr. That last sum equals the chart's 34,000 capacity for a still larger area, but here it is kitchen arithmetic, not a change of floor area.

Buying 30,000 for a shaded area that should have started at 24,000 and then dropped 10% leaves a unit that satisfies the thermostat before it dries the air. ENERGY STAR's phrase for that air is "cool, moist air will make the room feel damp and clammy."

The ceiling under these rows is 8-foot. Higher than that, select a room air conditioner with a higher CADR. The chart does not print a replacement capacity for the taller room.

Frequently asked questions

What size air conditioner for 1,500 sq ft?

1,400 up to 1,500 sq ft is 24,000 BTU/hr. 1,500 up to 2,000 sq ft is 30,000 BTU/hr. Heavy shade supports the 24,000 start. Sun or more than two people regularly supports the 30,000 start.

How large is that boundary step?

30,000 − 24,000 = 6,000 BTU/hr. That difference equals the entire capacity of the 150 up to 250 sq ft row. Ten percent of 24,000 is 2,400, so the shaded lower path is 21,600 BTU/hr.

What if someone buys 30,000 BTU/hr for a shaded 1,500 sq ft area?

That skips the lower row and the shade cut. ENERGY STAR says a unit that is too big cools the room before it removes humidity, and "cool, moist air will make the room feel damp and clammy." The sunny upper path is the one that starts at 30,000 and then adds 10% to reach 33,000 BTU/hr.

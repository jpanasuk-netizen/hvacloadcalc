What size mini-split do I need for each room?

Short answer: 9k for bedrooms, 12k for living rooms, 18k–24k for open plans — and the math below tells you when to break those rules.

Quick answer: A 9,000 BTU head covers almost every bedroom and a 12,000 BTU head covers almost every living room. Going smaller is false economy; going bigger just costs money and dehumidifies worse.

The head sizes you can actually buy

Mini-split indoor heads come in fixed capacities: 9k, 12k, 15k, 18k, 24k, 30k, 36k BTU/hr (the "k" means thousands). You size up to the nearest head at or above your calculated load. Use the room BTU calculator for the load, this table for the match:

The workhorse answer: a 9,000 BTU head covers almost every bedroom, and a 12,000 covers almost every living room. If a salesperson jumps you two sizes on a normal room, ask them to show the load calc.

Multi-zone vs several single-zone units

For two or three rooms, compare before defaulting to multi-zone. A single-zone 12k condenser + head runs ~$1,500–2,500 DIY-friendly; a two-zone condenser with two heads typically runs $4,000–6,000 installed. Multi-zone wins when outdoor space is limited or rooms need simultaneous different setpoints. But note: multi-zone condensers divide their capacity across active heads, and if all zones call at once each head gets less than its nameplate — one reason pros size multi-zone systems to the sum of room loads with attention to diversity, not just head count.

The oversizing trap, quantified

Fixed-speed AC dehumidifies in the last half of each cycle, after the thermostat is nearly satisfied. Oversize the unit and it satisfies the thermostat first, every time — cold, clammy air, and a compressor that cycles on and off instead of cruising. Inverter mini-splits throttle down instead of shutting off, which is why they forgive 10–20% oversizing. Past ~30%, even inverters short-cycle at minimum output and you've paid for capacity you can't use.

DIY vs pro install

Pre-charged "no-vacuum" DIY units (Mr Cool-style) have made single-zone installs genuinely doable — hang the head, drill one 3-inch hole, connect the line set, 240 V or 120 V circuit by an electrician. Pro-installed brands (Mitsubishi, Fujitsu, Daikin) still outperform on efficiency and longevity, and multi-zone systems are pro territory: refrigerant handling, vacuum pull, and charge weighing are not DIY skills.

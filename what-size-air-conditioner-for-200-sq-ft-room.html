What Size Air Conditioner Do I Need for a 200 Sq Ft Room?

200 sq ft is inside the 6,000 BTU/hr band, not on a line between two capacities.

Full breakdown: BTU per square foot.

Quick answer: A 200 sq ft room sits inside ENERGY STAR's 150 up to 250 sq ft row, so the chart capacity is 6,000 BTU/hr. Shade, sun, extra people, and a kitchen change that figure. They do not move 200 sq ft onto the 5,000 or 7,000 row.

Confirm the area before you trust the row. Multiply length by width. A 200 sq ft result is not the 100 up to 150 row (5,000 BTU/hr) and it is not the 250 up to 300 row (7,000 BTU/hr). Only a measurement that actually crosses 150 or 250 sq ft changes the row. Shade is not a substitute for that measurement.

How to size it

Start at 6,000 BTU/hr and apply the adjustment that matches the room. Use shade or sun, not both. Add people only when more than two people are in the room regularly. Add the kitchen figure only when the unit is used in a kitchen.

AdjustmentOn the 6,000 rowSource
Heavily shaded, reduce capacity 10%6,000 − 10% = 5,400 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Very sunny, increase capacity 10%6,000 + 10% = 6,600 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Each additional person beyond two6,000 + 600 BTU/hr = 6,600 BTU/hrENERGY STAR
Unit used in a kitchen6,000 + 4,000 BTU/hr = 10,000 BTU/hrENERGY STAR

10% of 6,000 BTU/hr is 600 BTU/hr (6,000 × 10%).

On this particular row, 10% of 6,000 is 600 BTU/hr, which is the same count as the 600 BTU/hr person adder. They are not the same step. A shaded 200 sq ft room with only the usual occupancy is 6,000 − 10% = 5,400 BTU/hr. A sunny 200 sq ft room is 6,000 + 10% = 6,600 BTU/hr. A sunny room that also has one person beyond two is 6,000 + 10% + 600, which is 6,600 + 600 = 7,200 BTU/hr. A kitchen use of the same row is a different jump: 6,000 + 4,000 = 10,000 BTU/hr.

Installation matters at this size because the unit is small and the sash gap is a large share of its airflow. ENERGY STAR says an improperly installed room air conditioner leaks as much air as a 6-square-inch hole. Foam around the sash is the accessory that matches that warning.

Every figure above assumes the chart's 8-foot ceiling. For a taller room, ENERGY STAR does not publish a foot-by-foot adder. The note is to select a room air conditioner with a higher CADR.

Room-AC chart: 6,000 BTU/hr is a room air conditioner size. It is not a whole-house size. Size a central system with a load calculation on the HVAC load calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What size air conditioner does a 200 sq ft room need?

ENERGY STAR puts 150 up to 250 sq ft at 6,000 BTU/hr. A 200 sq ft room is inside that band, so the starting capacity is 6,000 BTU/hr, not the 5,000 row below it and not the 7,000 row above it.

Does shade drop a 200 sq ft room to 5,000 BTU/hr?

No. Heavy shade reduces the chosen row by 10%. Ten percent of 6,000 is 600, and 6,000 − 600 = 5,400 BTU/hr. The room stays on the 150 up to 250 row; the percent does not move it into 100 up to 150.

How much do people and a kitchen add in a 200 sq ft room?

Each person beyond two adds 600 BTU/hr, so 6,000 + 600 = 6,600 BTU/hr. A unit used in a kitchen adds 4,000 BTU/hr, so 6,000 + 4,000 = 10,000 BTU/hr. On this row the shade swing and the person adder are both 600 BTU/hr, and they still count separately.

What if the window unit leaks around the sash?

The chart assumes an 8-foot ceiling. Separately, ENERGY STAR says an improperly installed room air conditioner leaks as much air as a 6-square-inch hole. A higher ceiling is a reason to select a room air conditioner with a higher CADR, and a central system still belongs on the HVAC load calculator.
